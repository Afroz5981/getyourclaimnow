What Is the Roundup Lawsuit?

Roundup is the world's most widely used herbicide, containing the active ingredient glyphosate. It has been used in homes, farms, schools, and parks for decades by millions of Americans.

The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) classified glyphosate as a probable human carcinogen. Scientific studies have linked regular Roundup exposure to Non-Hodgkin's Lymphoma and other serious blood cancers.

Billions of dollars have already been paid to settle Roundup claims — and litigation is still actively ongoing for thousands of victims yet to be compensated. If you or a loved one used Roundup and were later diagnosed with cancer, you may have a valid legal claim.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Roundup and why is it dangerous?+
Roundup is a widely used herbicide containing glyphosate. The World Health Organization's cancer research agency classified glyphosate as a probable human carcinogen. Studies have linked regular exposure to Non-Hodgkin's Lymphoma and other cancers, particularly in farmers, landscapers, and gardeners who used it regularly over many years.
What cancers are linked to Roundup exposure?+
The primary cancer linked to Roundup is Non-Hodgkin's Lymphoma (NHL), including Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL), Follicular Lymphoma, and Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL). If you have been diagnosed with any blood cancer and have a history of Roundup exposure, you may have a valid claim.
How long do I have to file a Roundup claim?+
Deadlines vary by state, but most states allow 2–3 years from your cancer diagnosis. It is critical that you act quickly — contact us today for a free consultation to ensure you don't miss your window to file.
Do I need to pay anything upfront?+
Absolutely not. Our attorneys work on a contingency fee basis — you pay nothing unless you win. Your initial case evaluation is 100% free with no hidden fees or obligations.
What if I only used Roundup occasionally?+
Even occasional use may qualify depending on the frequency and duration of exposure. Submit a free evaluation and our attorneys will review your specific situation at no cost.
