What Is the Depo-Provera Lawsuit?

Depo-Provera is a brand-name injectable contraceptive containing medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA), used by millions of women for birth control. It is typically injected every three months.

A landmark 2024 study published in the British Medical Journal found that women who used Depo-Provera for more than one year had a five to seven times higher risk of developing meningioma — a type of intracranial tumor that can cause seizures, vision loss, headaches, and cognitive problems requiring surgery.

Despite the growing body of evidence, the manufacturer failed to adequately warn patients about this serious risk. Women who were harmed may now file lawsuits seeking compensation for medical bills, pain and suffering, and more.

  • Linked to meningioma (intracranial brain tumor)
  • Risk increases significantly with long-term use (1+ year)
  • May require surgery, radiation, or ongoing monitoring
  • Manufacturer failed to adequately warn patients

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a meningioma and how is it linked to Depo-Provera?+
A meningioma is a tumor that forms on the membranes surrounding the brain and spinal cord. A 2024 British Medical Journal study found women using Depo-Provera for over a year had a 5–7x higher risk of developing this tumor compared to non-users, due to the synthetic progesterone (MPA) stimulating tumor growth.
Do I qualify if I no longer use Depo-Provera?+
Yes. Many claimants used Depo-Provera in the past. As long as you used the injectable and were later diagnosed with a meningioma or brain tumor, you may still qualify to file a claim.
